The reputation

Known for centuries as witch country

Siquijor's old name — Isla del Fuego, Island of Fire — came from fireflies so dense early Spanish sailors mistook the coastline for flame. That mystique never left: the island is still the Philippines' best-known seat of folk healers, herbal medicine, and Holy Week potion-brewing rituals passed down through generations.

The reality

One of the Visayas' calmest islands to visit

Underneath the folklore is a quiet, low-development island with some of the clearest water in the region, a single coastal highway that makes self-driving easy, and a pace of life built around waterfalls, dive sites, and towns that still close for siesta. The witchcraft is heritage, not hazard.

Nature

Three-tier waterfalls

Cambugahay Falls drops through turquoise pools connected by stone steps, with a rope swing locals treat as a rite of passage. Go before 9am to beat the tour vans.

More
Folklore

A 400-year-old balete tree

Roots wide enough to climb, shading a cold spring where small fish nibble your feet while you sit. One of the most photographed spots on the island, and still a living tree in daily use by locals.

More
Coast

Cliff jumps and white sand

Salagdoong Beach pairs two built diving platforms with calm coves for swimming. Paliton Beach, on the west side, keeps its sand powder-fine and almost undeveloped for sunset.

More
After dark

Firefly-lit mangrove rivers

Paddled boats glide up tidal rivers just after dusk, when thousands of fireflies synchronise their glow along the branches. No lights, no engines — the origin of the island's old name.

More
Heritage

Coral-stone churches

Lazi's St. Isidore Labrador Parish Church faces a 1887 convent built from coral stone — believed the largest of its kind in Asia, and still in daily use.

More
Culture

Living folk healing

Manghihilot healers practice herbal massage and bone-setting passed down through generations, most visible during the Holy Week potion-brewing ritual. Respected trade, not a tourist show — go through a local introduction.

Before you book

Short answers to what people ask most.

No — it's cultural heritage, not a safety issue. Siquijor is considered one of the more relaxed, low-crime islands in the Visayas. The folk-healing tradition is respected locally, and visitors are welcome to learn about it through a guesthouse introduction.

Three full days covers the loop comfortably — one day near the port, one for the south (falls and heritage), one for the west coast. Two days works if you skip the cave or the nature park; a week suits anyone adding diving.

Most rental shops ask for any valid driver's license, but an International Driving Permit is the safer bet and some insurers require it. Helmets are provided and legally required.

Cash. ATMs exist in the capital and Larena but can run out on weekends — withdraw what you need for the whole trip before heading to smaller towns, and keep small bills for entrance fees.
